# Cross-document audit

**Scope:** Current PRD, architecture spine, and brief, with emphasis on Timed Pause: 5/10/30-minute choices; frozen phase time and preserved remaining duration; same-phase resume at pause end; app foreground/open and one haptic; no phase advancement; and early manual Resume cancelling the Wakeup and rendering stale callbacks inert. Checked FR/AD/UJ references, capability map, MVP, success metrics, and device acceptance tests.

**Verdict: Conditional pass.** The three documents consistently specify the main Timed Pause behavior, and the PRD and architecture define early-resume cancellation and stale-callback behavior. Cross-document traceability and verification details still have gaps: the brief omits early-resume/stale-callback behavior; architecture capability-map bindings omit the timed-pause signal and phase-semantics decisions; the architecture spine's `binds` metadata is incomplete; and device acceptance does not explicitly assert that a stale callback cannot reopen the app. The Pebble Time 2 Wakeup, haptic, and foreground behavior remains an unpassed pre-implementation gate.

## Residual mismatches

- **Brief omits cancellation semantics:** PRD FR-5 requires early manual Resume to cancel the pause-end Wakeup and requires an already-delivered stale callback to cause neither haptic nor UI reopen (FR-5 consequences; repeated at §4.2). Architecture AD-17 specifies cancellation, generation increment, and rejection of callbacks with neither haptic nor app reopen. The brief specifies timed pause and pause-end behavior but says nothing about early Resume or stale callbacks (§Executive Summary, Success Criteria, Scope, and Open Questions). Its success criteria therefore do not cover this control/race case.
- **Capability map omits governing decisions:** Architecture map rows for FR-5 and UJ-2 cite AD-1–AD-4, AD-9–AD-12, AD-16–AD-17, but omit AD-6 (phase/control semantics) and AD-7 (haptic plus app-open/UI behavior). The timed-pause rules are present in those decisions, but the map does not trace the capabilities to them.
- **Architecture frontmatter bindings are stale/incomplete:** `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` frontmatter lists `binds: [FR-1, FR-2, FR-3, FR-4, FR-5, FR-6, UJ-1]`, omitting FR-7 and UJ-2 even though the spine defines FR-7-related architecture and AD-1/AD-9 bind UJ-2. This weakens document-level traceability, including the timed-pause journey.
- **Device acceptance misses the stale-reopen assertion:** Architecture §Deferred calls for early manual resume followed by stale-callback rejection “with no haptic,” but does not explicitly assert no app reopen/foreground request. PRD FR-5 and AD-17 require both no haptic and no reopen. Add the no-reopen assertion to the device acceptance case for full behavioral coverage.
- **Haptic delivery language differs in strength:** PRD FR-5 and the brief describe one haptic at normal pause end; architecture AD-16 says emit one, while AD-12 defines a one-haptic request as an at-most-once, best-effort API attempt. PRD FR-5 allows at most one on overdue recovery. This is consistent on preventing duplicates, but “one haptic” can imply guaranteed physical delivery beyond the architecture's best-effort contract.
- **MVP/platform-gate framing differs slightly:** PRD MVP includes timed pause and makes background reminders subject to its §5.1 acceptance criterion; architecture requires revisiting the feature if timed-pause Wakeup foreground/haptic support fails. The brief includes timed pause in first-version scope and requires verification, but does not explicitly state the same revisit condition. The three documents agree that implementation depends on physical verification, but the brief does not fully express the contingency.

## Checked and aligned

- **Timed Pause core:** All three documents specify exactly 5, 10, or 30 minutes; phase time freezes; remaining duration is preserved; pause end resumes the same phase, opens/foregrounds the app to show phase and remaining time, emits one haptic, and does not advance the cycle. References: PRD UJ-2, FR-5; architecture AD-2, AD-6, AD-7, AD-16; brief Executive Summary, Success Criteria, Scope, and decision bullet.
- **PRD identifiers and internal traceability:** FR-5 is the Timed Pause requirement and UJ-2 is its journey. PRD SM-1/SM-2/SM-3/SM-4 cover timed-pause completion, preserved remaining time, no advancement, overdue recovery, and early-resume cancellation of the haptic. SM-4 does not explicitly measure stale app reopen; see residual above. PRD FR-5 consequence coverage includes stale reopen rejection.
- **Architecture decision identifiers:** AD-16 covers timed-pause interval, frozen phase clock, same-phase resume, pause-end foreground/open and haptic, and no phase advance. AD-17 covers manual-resume cancellation and stale callback suppression. AD-10/AD-11 define serialization, generation, Wakeup ID, and callback validity. Their FR-5/UJ-2 bindings are present within the decisions.
- **MVP scope:** PRD §7.1 and brief Scope include timed pause with the same 5/10/30-minute semantics; architecture covers it in AD-16/AD-17, the capability map, and its pre-implementation gate. The only scope discrepancy is how explicitly the brief states the feature contingency if device support fails.
- **Device tests:** Architecture §Deferred tests 5/10/30-minute timed pause, preserved remaining duration, one pause-end haptic, manual resume before pause end, stale callback rejection, overdue pause-end recovery, and device foregrounding/haptics. PRD §5.1 separately requires target-device verification of timed-pause Wakeup haptic and app foreground/open after leaving the app. The stale callback test needs the explicit no-reopen assertion noted above.
- **Other prior audit issue:** The earlier audit's invalid-time residual is no longer supported by the current primary artifacts: PRD §5.2, architecture AD-10/AD-12 and §Deferred, and brief §Open Questions define the invalid-time behavior and leave timing tolerance/device validation open. UTC and invalid-time policy otherwise align.
